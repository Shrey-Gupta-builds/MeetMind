QUESTION 1: LLM PATTERNS & CONTEXT LIMITS
Q: "What is Map-Reduce summarization and why use it instead of passing the
   entire transcript to Mistral?"

Interviewer is testing: Advanced prompt engineering patterns, token limits, and
   LLM attention behavior.

IDEAL ANSWER:
"Map-Reduce is a distributed summarization pattern designed to handle long-form
documents:
1. Map Step: The transcript is split into 3,000-character chunks. Each chunk is
   sent to the LLM independently with a prompt: 'Summarize this portion
   concisely.'
2. Reduce (Combine) Step: All individual chunk summaries are concatenated and
   sent to a final synthesis prompt: 'Combine these partial summaries into one
   final professional meeting summary in bullet points.'
Why not stuff the whole transcript into a single prompt?
- Context Window Limits: Very long meetings (1-2 hours) can exceed model
  context limits.
- The 'Lost in the Middle' Phenomenon: Research proves that LLMs retrieve
  information best at the very beginning and very end of their context window.
  Stuffing 20,000 tokens causes the LLM to ignore crucial decisions buried in
  the middle. Map-Reduce guarantees equal attention to all sections."

QUESTION 2: LANGCHAIN LCEL (LANGCHAIN EXPRESSION LANGUAGE)
Q: "How does the pipeline syntax `prompt | llm | StrOutputParser()` work in
   LangChain LCEL?"

Interviewer is testing: Your modern LangChain knowledge (LCEL vs legacy
   deprecated LLMChain).

IDEAL ANSWER:
"LCEL relies on the `Runnable` protocol, inspired by Unix pipe syntax (`|`):
1. Every component in LCEL implements the `Runnable` interface (providing
   `.invoke()`, `.batch()`, `.stream()`, and `.ainvoke()`).
2. When chaining `prompt | llm | StrOutputParser()`:
   - The dictionary input is passed into `ChatPromptTemplate`, which formats
     prompt messages (`PromptValue`).
   - `PromptValue` is piped directly into the `ChatMistralAI` model, which
     generates a `ChatResult` (AIMessage).
   - `StrOutputParser` extracts the pure text string from the AIMessage.
3. Benefits: LCEL natively supports asynchronous execution, parallel batching,
   streaming, and easy integration with observability tools (LangSmith)."

QUESTION 3: TEXT CHUNKING STRATEGY
Q: "Why did you choose `RecursiveCharacterTextSplitter` with chunk_size=3000 and
   overlap=200 here, but chunk_size=500 in vector_store.py?"

Interviewer is testing: Difference between summarization chunking vs RAG
   retrieval chunking.

IDEAL ANSWER:
"Summarization and RAG have fundamentally different context requirements:
- For Summarization (chunk_size=3000): We want high semantic breadth. The LLM
  needs enough surrounding conversation (~600-750 words) to understand
  context, who is speaking, and the flow of discussion. Overlap of 200
  characters prevents concepts from being cleanly sliced at arbitrary character
  counts.
- For RAG Retrieval (chunk_size=500): We want high semantic precision. If a
  chunk is 3,000 characters, the embedding vector gets diluted across many
  disparate topics. A tight 500-character chunk (~100 words) creates a dense
  vector embedding that matches specific user questions with high similarity
  scores."

QUESTION 4: MODEL SELECTION & PARAMETERS
Q: "Why did you pick Mistral (`mistral-small-latest`) with temperature=0.3?"

Interviewer is testing: Model selection criteria, pricing, and hyperparameter
   tuning.

IDEAL ANSWER:
"1. Model Selection: Mistral offers top-tier reasoning capabilities at a
   fraction of the latency and API cost of GPT-4. `mistral-small-latest` is
   optimized for low-latency reasoning and structured output.
2. Temperature = 0.3: Temperature controls the randomness/creativity of token
   sampling. For creative storytelling, you use 0.7-1.0. For meeting
   summarization, we want high factual consistency and low hallucination, so
   temperature is kept low (0.3), but slightly above 0.0 to maintain natural,
   varied prose."

QUESTION 5: TITLE GENERATION
Q: "In `generate_title()`, you passed `transcript[:2000]`. Why not the full
   transcript?"

Interviewer is testing: Efficiency, token cost, and domain understanding of
   human meetings.

IDEAL ANSWER:
"In almost all formal and informal meetings, the agenda, purpose, and topic
introduction are discussed in the first 2-5 minutes.
Passing `transcript[:2000]` (~400 words) provides complete context of the
meeting's purpose while consuming negligible tokens and returning the title in
under 1 second."
