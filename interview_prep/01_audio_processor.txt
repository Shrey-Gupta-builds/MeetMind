QUESTION 1: FUNDAMENTALS & SIGNAL PROCESSING
Q: "Why do you convert audio to 16,000 Hz (16kHz) Mono WAV? Why not pass the
   original MP3/MP4 directly?"

Interviewer is testing: Your knowledge of speech model architecture and audio
   preprocessing standards.

IDEAL ANSWER:
"Speech-to-Text models like Whisper are pre-trained on log-mel spectrograms
extracted from 16kHz, single-channel (mono) audio.
1. Multi-channel audio (stereo): Having left and right channels doubles the
   data payload without providing extra speech information. Downmixing to mono
   halves the audio memory footprint.
2. Sample Rate (16kHz): Human speech frequencies relevant to transcription lie
   mostly below 8kHz (Nyquist theorem requires double the frequency, hence
   16kHz). Higher sample rates like 44.1kHz (music CD standard) waste CPU/GPU
   memory and force the STT model to resample internally during feature
   extraction.
3. WAV Format: Uncompressed linear PCM (WAV) eliminates lossy decoding overhead
   during inference."


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
QUESTION 2: SYSTEM DESIGN & MEMORY MANAGEMENT
Q: "Why do you chunk audio into 10-minute segments instead of processing a
   1-hour recording in one go?"

Interviewer is testing: Memory (RAM/VRAM) management and model degradation edge
   cases.

IDEAL ANSWER:
"Passing long, uninterrupted audio directly to neural networks introduces two
major risks:
1. Out-Of-Memory (OOM) Crashes: Loading huge audio tensors into PyTorch exhausts
   RAM and GPU VRAM on standard infrastructure.
2. Attention Drift & Hallucination: Whisper operates internally with a 30-second
   attention window. On long continuous audio files, positional embeddings can
   drift, causing Whisper to get trapped in repetitive loops (repeating the
   same sentence indefinitely).
Slicing into 10-minute segments isolates failure domains, ensures predictable
memory consumption, and enables parallel processing across worker threads if
scaling."

 
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
QUESTION 3: CRITICAL THINKING & EDGE CASES
Q: "If you arbitrarily cut audio every 10 minutes, won't you cut words or
   sentences in half? How do you solve that?"

Interviewer is testing: Edge case identification and familiarity with
   production speech tools.

IDEAL ANSWER:
"Yes, fixed-time chunking can split a word or phoneme across chunk boundaries.
In our baseline, 10-minute chunks make this a rare event (only 5 cuts across a
1-hour meeting).
However, for production systems, the industry standard solution is:
- Voice Activity Detection (VAD) / Silence-Aware Chunking: Using libraries like
  `silero-vad` or `webrtcvad` to detect natural silence intervals (e.g., pauses
  > 500ms) and slicing only at sentence boundaries.
- Overlapping Chunk Windows: Adding a 1-2 second audio overlap between chunks
  and deduplicating boundary tokens during text stitching."


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
QUESTION 4: TOOLING & DEPENDENCY CHOICE
Q: "Why use `yt-dlp` instead of `pytube` for YouTube downloads?"

Interviewer is testing: Real-world engineering pragmatism vs. following
   outdated tutorials.

IDEAL ANSWER:
"`pytube` is often unmaintained and breaks frequently whenever YouTube updates
its player deciphering algorithms, causing persistent 403 Forbidden HTTP
errors.
`yt-dlp` is the industry-standard fork of youtube-dl. It is actively
maintained, handles YouTube rate-limiting and throttling, and natively
orchestrates FFmpeg postprocessors (`FFmpegExtractAudio`) to download and
transcode audio streams directly into high-quality WAV in a single operation."


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
QUESTION 5: SYSTEM PROFILING & BOTTLENECK ANALYSIS
Q: "Where is the latency bottleneck in this whole project: audio extraction,
   STT, or the LLM?"

Interviewer is testing: System-level intuition and performance profiling.

IDEAL ANSWER:
"The dominant bottleneck is Speech-to-Text (Transcription).
- Audio downloading and conversion: ~3 to 8 seconds.
- Vector search (ChromaDB): < 20 milliseconds.
- LLM Summarization (Mistral API): ~3 to 6 seconds.
- Local Whisper inference on CPU: Roughly 0.8x to 1.5x real-time (a 10-minute
  audio chunk takes 8-12 minutes on CPU).
To optimize this in production:
1. Run Whisper on GPU (CUDA / TensorRT).
2. Use `faster-whisper` (CTranslate2 implementation), which provides a 4x
  speedup with 50% lower VRAM footprint via 8-bit quantization."
